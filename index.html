research line sticker

Posted on 2026-11-20

1. Start from a conversation moment. Write down a real situation where people in Taiwan chat, like replying to a boss, a couple saying goodnight, a family group, or complaining about work. Then list 8 lines people actually type in it. If you can’t get to 8 easily, the idea is too narrow.

2. Search the store with those words. Open the LINE Sticker Shop and search the theme and 2 or 3 of the lines, for example 社畜, 婉拒 or 已讀. For each search, note three things:

  • roughly how many results there are;
  • how good the top 10 look;
  • whether the top results are official brands or individual creators.

3. Read what you find.

  • Gap: few results, or the ones there are look old, ugly or off-topic. This is the best case.
  • Proven but beatable: lots of results, and the top ones are by creators and look average. Demand is real and you can compete on style.
  • Skip: the top results are all brands, celebrities or famous characters. You’d be paying for their exposure.

4. Check the rankings for format, not topics. Look through the store’s top-selling and rising lists for Taiwan. For each of 10 entries, note:

  • whether it has captions;
  • how big the face is;
  • whether it’s daily replies or a theme;
  • whether it’s one character or a pair.

Copy the pattern that keeps showing up, never the character.

5. Test the idea on yourself. Go through your own LINE chats from the past week and count the moments you would have sent this sticker. Zero means probably not.

6. Score it from 1 to 3 on each of these:

  • how often people would use it;
  • how open the search results are;
  • how much you like it.

Make anything that scores 7 or more. The “how much you like it” score is what keeps your own taste in the process.

7. Keep a record. After 60 days, compare Taiwan sales against your scores, leaving out your own gift purchases. Within a few months you’ll see which kind of score predicts sales for you, and that becomes your own research method.

Steps 2 to 4 are the slow part, and I can do them for each idea and hand you the scores. You’d only do steps 1, 5 and the “how much you like it” score.

my-fav-subgent-spell

Posted on 2026-06-20

Take these N feature requests and implement them in parallel using independent subagents, one per feature branch. For each: (1) write a short spec, (2) write failing tests first, (3) implement until all tests pass without modifying unrelated files, (4) run a self-review that flags any change not directly tied to the stated goal and reverts scope creep, (5) commit only the intended files and open a PR. Keep the full existing test suite green for every branch. Summarize each PR and any tradeoffs at the end.
